Mathematics Made Simple: A Practical Guide for SS3 Students

By Ezeikpe Akachukwu Kingsley (Kabaka)September 30, 20266 min read

For many secondary school students, Mathematics is one of the subjects that creates the greatest anxiety. A question may look complicated before you even begin, and seeing several numbers, symbols and formulas on one page can make you feel as though you do not know where to start.

But Mathematics becomes easier when you stop treating every question as a new problem and begin understanding the principles behind it. You do not need to know every answer immediately. You need to learn how to approach a question, identify what it requires and work through it logically.

1. Understand the question before solving it

One common mistake students make is rushing to calculate before understanding what the question is asking.

Before solving a Mathematics question, identify three things:

  • What information has the question given you?
  • What are you expected to find?
  • Which mathematical rule or formula connects the information to the answer?

This simple habit can help you avoid using the wrong formula or performing calculations that do not answer the question.

2. Learn the principle, not just the formula

Formulas are useful, but memorising them without understanding their meaning can become a problem when an examination question is presented differently from the examples you have practised.

For example, consider the formula for the area of a rectangle:

Area = length × breadth

If a rectangle has a length of 8 cm and a breadth of 5 cm, its area is:

Area = 8 × 5
Area = 40 cm²

The important point is that you are multiplying two measurements to determine the space covered by the rectangle. Once you understand this, you are better prepared to solve questions that use different measurements.

3. Break difficult questions into smaller steps

You do not have to solve an entire question in your head. Write down each step and move forward carefully.

Consider this simple equation:

2x + 6 = 16

Step 1: Subtract 6 from both sides.

2x = 16 - 6
2x = 10

Step 2: Divide both sides by 2.

x = 10 ÷ 2
x = 5

Answer: x = 5.

Notice that we did not try to solve everything at once. We performed one operation, simplified the equation and then performed the next operation. This approach is useful in algebra and many other areas of Mathematics.

4. Practise the way you will be examined

Preparing for WAEC, NECO or JAMB requires more than reading notes and memorising formulas. You also need to practise answering questions.

After learning a topic, solve questions on that topic without immediately checking the answers. When you make a mistake, identify why it happened. Did you misunderstand the question, choose the wrong formula, make a calculation error or forget an important rule?

Keep a record of the mistakes you frequently make. Revisiting those mistakes can help you recognise patterns and improve your accuracy.

5. Build your foundation before rushing ahead

Mathematical topics are connected. If you struggle with fractions, basic algebra or negative numbers, more advanced topics may become unnecessarily difficult.

When a topic seems impossible, go back to the simpler concept it depends on. Strengthening your foundation is not a sign of weakness. It is a practical way to make progress.

Study one concept, work through examples, practise similar questions and move to a more difficult level when you can explain the method yourself.

A simple challenge for you

Try solving this equation without looking at the worked example above:

3x + 4 = 19

Subtract 4 from both sides, then divide the result by 3. If you followed the steps correctly, you should obtain:

x = 5

If you got it right, try another question with different numbers. If you got it wrong, return to the steps and identify where your calculation changed.

Final thoughts

Mathematics is a skill that develops through understanding, practice and patience. You may not understand every topic immediately, but that does not mean you cannot improve.

Whether you are preparing for WAEC, NECO or JAMB, concentrate on understanding the principles behind questions, practising consistently and learning from your mistakes.

Do not be afraid of a difficult Mathematics question. Break it down, take it one step at a time and give yourself the opportunity to understand it.
